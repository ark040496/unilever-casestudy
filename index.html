Overview

What is the Recipe Management System?

The Recipe Management System (RMS) is the internal tool where Unilever teams build recipes, track groceries and yields, and send each recipe for sign-off to the right regional market. Restaurant managers use it in the gaps between busy kitchen shifts, so every minute in the tool matters.

Problem statement

What wasn’t working

The legacy tool made a simple job slow. Managers jumped between tabs for basic details, found mistakes only after submitting, and met components that looked and behaved differently from page to page. On a phone or tablet, the tables simply clipped.

Details spread across tabsErrors found after submitHard-coded coloursMismatched status tagsClipped tables on handheldsNo mobile layout

Opportunity

Bring the recipe and its market administration onto one split-pane screen, with errors caught before sign-off.

Replace hard-coded styling with a token library, so every screen is consistent and engineering can build from it.

Research and discovery

Listening first, between busy shifts

Before designing anything, I audited the legacy tool against Unilever’s brand guidelines. Then I interviewed restaurant managers one to one, asking how they really check stock, calculate yields and submit recipes. Filter the findings below to see how each one shaped the new interface.

4

bottlenecks surfaced from the audit and the manager interviews: fragmented details, late error discovery, inconsistent components and no mobile readiness.

Key insights

01

Fragmented grocery and recipe details

Before: managers jumped across separate tabs to check ingredients, yield sizes and market permissions.
After: a split-pane layout pairs Recipe Overview and Yield Details with Admin sign-off on one screen.

02

Late error discovery before sign-off

Before: missing required fields or character overruns only appeared after clicking submit.
After: live character counters (for example 24 characters), draft badges and pre-signoff validation modals.

03

Inconsistent legacy components

Before: hard-coded colours, uneven input borders and mismatched status tags slowed engineering down.
After: 220 Figma Variables map brand colours to semantic and component-level tokens.

04

No mobile or tablet readiness

Before: managers checking stock on handheld devices faced clipped tables and horizontal scrolling.
After: responsive desktop and mobile viewports across Login, recipe tables and forms.

      Design decisions and impact

      Decisions that turned a slow tool into a quick one

      • Split-pane layout: recipe content on the left, market governance on the right, so nothing is a tab away.
      • Early feedback: live character counters, draft badges and a pre-signoff check surface errors while there is still time to fix them.
      • Tokens before screens: every field, chip and panel draws from one library, so the visuals stay consistent as the screens scale.
      • Desktop and mobile together: Login, recipe tables and forms designed responsively from the start.

      What I took from this

      Building the tokens before the screens meant Week 4 was assembly rather than invention. And sitting with managers early kept me honest about the real constraint: they open this tool in short gaps between shifts, so every screen had to be quick to scan.
